ShiftSense Handbook

Every role, screen, workflow and rule in the ShiftSense proof of concept, in plain language. Each technical term is explained the first time it matters, and again in the glossary. The last sections list the gaps we found, how each was resolved, and what is still open.

Current as of 18 Oct 2026 · 459 automated tests passing · all figures come from a simulated, seeded dataset, not EXL data.

What ShiftSense is

ShiftSense helps a call centre keep the right number of skilled people on the phones at every moment. Call centres promise clients a service level (for example, "80% of calls answered within 20 seconds"). To keep that promise, the centre must have enough agents, trained for the right type of call, working at each half-hour of the day.

ShiftSense does four things continuously:

  • Predicts how many calls will arrive in each half-hour, and turns that into how many people are needed.
  • Plans a two-week roster (who works which shift on which queue) that meets the need without breaking any working-time rule.
  • Detects problems as they happen: someone is sick, doesn't show up, leaves early, or calls spike.
  • Recovers by proposing the smallest, cheapest change that closes the gap, for example moving a trained colleague, swapping shifts or offering overtime. A person approves every change.
Predict→Plan→Execute→Detect→Recover→Learn

The question the proof of concept answers: can ShiftSense build a better roster than a careful manual process, and restore coverage faster and at lower extra cost when the roster breaks, without ever breaking a hard working-time rule?

Three promises that run through the whole product.
  1. People decide; the system recommends. No roster changes unless an authorised person approves. The AI never decides anything.
  2. Nothing is invented. A figure the system doesn't have (for example forecast accuracy, a budget, or a penalty in rupees) is shown as "Unavailable" or "Not configured", never estimated.
  3. Permissions are enforced on the server. Hiding a button is never the only protection: every action is re-checked against the person's role, team and identity when it is performed.

Terms explained

Grouped by topic. Where a term is used later in the handbook it means exactly what is written here.

Call-centre and staffing terms

Queue
A type of call, defined as process × language. There are four: Claims-EN, Claims-HI (Hindi), Banking-EN, Banking-HI. Every call waits in one queue.
Primary queue / secondary queue
Each agent has one main (primary) queue. About 30% are also trained for one or more other (secondary) queues. An agent is never placed on a queue they are not trained for, anywhere in the system.
Cross-skilled
Trained for more than one queue, so they can be moved to cover a gap elsewhere.
Interval
A half-hour slot. All demand, staffing and coverage is calculated per queue per half-hour (48 intervals a day).
Service level (SLA)
The promise to the client, written as "X% of calls answered within Y seconds". Here: Claims-EN and Banking-EN 80% in 20 s, open 24 h; Claims-HI and Banking-HI 80% in 30 s, open 09:00–21:00. SLA stands for service-level agreement.
AHT (average handle time)
How long one call takes on average, talking plus wrap-up. Longer calls need more people.
Forecast
The predicted number of calls per queue per half-hour. ShiftSense uses a simple, transparent method: the average of the same weekday over the last 4 weeks. There is no machine learning.
Uncertainty band
A range around the forecast showing how far real calls might reasonably differ. Here it is ±1.28 standard deviations, which covers about 80% of normal days.
Erlang C
A standard call-centre formula that turns "calls expected" plus "average call length" plus "service target" into "agents needed on the phones at once".
Shrinkage
The share of paid time when an agent is not available for calls (breaks, training, meetings). Set at 30%, so the required number of people is raised to cover it.
Required heads
The number of agents who should be working in a queue in a half-hour, after Erlang C and shrinkage.
Coverage %
Of all the required people-time, the share the roster actually provides. 100% means no shortfall.
People-hours
The unit for staffing gaps. One person short for one hour is 1 people-hour. One person short for one half-hour is 0.5.
Gap / shortage
Required minus scheduled, where scheduled is lower. Measured in people-hours.
Surplus
More people scheduled than required.
SLA exposure
The gap weighted by how sensitive that queue is to missing its service target. A gap on a strict 20-second queue counts for more than the same gap on a 30-second queue.
Severity
How urgent an alert is: Critical At risk Healthy. It combines the gap, SLA sensitivity, forecast uncertainty, skill scarcity (how few trained replacements exist), time until the gap starts, and whether any recovery option exists.
Absenteeism
The share of scheduled shifts lost to absence or no-shows.

Roster and rule terms

Roster
The schedule: for every agent and day, which shift (or none) and which queue.
Shift
9 hours including a 1-hour break, so 8 paid. Morning 06–15, Day 09–18, Evening 14–23, Night 22–07. Shown as M, D, E, N.
Extension
Staying up to 2 hours longer, or starting up to 2 hours earlier, on a shift. Counts as overtime.
Overtime (OT)
Paid hours beyond the normal shift. Paid at 2× the hourly rate in this simulation.
Hard rule
A rule that may never be broken: trained queues only, one shift a day, at least 11 hours rest between shifts, at most 50 paid hours and 6 shifts a week, approved leave respected, nights only for night-permitted agents (at most 4 a week), at most 1 overtime day a fortnight, at most an 11-hour working span with extensions of at most 2 hours.
Soft preference
Something the optimiser tries for but may trade off, such as keeping people on their primary queue, fairness of nights and weekends, and personal shift preferences.
Rule checker
An independent piece of code that re-checks a whole roster against every hard rule after every plan, every recovery and every change. If it finds a violation, the change is refused.
Freeze window
Days that have already started cannot be re-planned or have requests approved for them.
Optimiser (CP-SAT)
The planning engine. CP-SAT is Google's open-source solver (part of OR-Tools). It searches millions of possible rosters for the one that best balances shortage, overstaffing, overtime, fairness, preferences and number of changes, while obeying every hard rule. It is limited to 60 seconds.
Manual heuristic baseline
A copy of how an analyst builds a roster by hand: apply the rules, fill the busiest half-hours first, spread skills, rotate nights and weekends, handle exceptions one by one. ShiftSense is measured against it.
Plan / draft / validated / published / superseded
A plan is a version of the roster. A draft is a proposed one. Validated means the rule checker passed it. Published means it is the live roster everyone works to. When a new plan is published, the old one becomes superseded and is kept unchanged for history.
Live roster
The published plan plus every approved change since (absences, swaps, approved fixes).
Roster fingerprint / stale draft
A fingerprint is a short code that changes whenever the live roster changes. Every draft remembers the fingerprint it was built from. If the live roster changed since (for example an absence was approved), the draft is stale and publishing it is refused, so nobody's approved change is silently overwritten.

Detection and recovery terms

Alert (incident)
A record created when something threatens coverage: an absence, a no-show, someone leaving early, or a call spike. It carries the gap, exposure, severity and a recommended fix.
No-show
A scheduled agent who never arrived. In the live workspace this is only ever recorded by a person; a missing check-in alone never becomes a no-show.
Call spike
Calls above forecast for a few hours, reported by Ops.
Recovery engine
Code that builds candidate fixes for a gap, removes any that break a rule, simulates the rest, ranks them and recommends the best. Steps: Generate → Filter → Simulate → Rank → Approve → Re-solve.
Cross-skill redeployment
Moving a trained agent from another queue for part of their shift. The queue they leave (the donor queue) must still meet its own requirement; this is called "protected".
Shift swap
Two agents exchange shifts. The partner must be trained for the same queue, and rules are checked for both.
Open shift
A shift offered to several trained agents. The first to accept gets it; the offer closes for the others.
Blast radius
How many people, shifts and queues a fix touches. Smaller is better, because fewer people's plans are disturbed.
Ranking
Fixes are ordered by extra cost, plus the service risk that would remain, plus blast radius. The smallest change that works is preferred.
Escalation
When the team lead cannot close a gap, they send the alert to Ops for a decision.
Residual / carried risk
Gap that remains after a decision. If Ops decides without adding cover (for example "accept the risk for this window"), the uncovered people-hours stay on record as carried risk and are never counted as covered.
Time-to-cover
How long, in simulated minutes, from an alert being raised until the gap is actually closed in the roster.

Product and technical terms

Live workspace
The real working copy: the published roster and genuine records made by people.
Demo scenario
A separate copy loaded with a prepared story for demonstrations. Nothing done in it changes the live workspace. Every screen shows a banner while you view it. Its records carry a run id starting sim-.
Run id
A label that ties every record (audit, notifications, history) to one workspace, so live and demo records never mix.
Live clock / simulation clock
There is no real-time clock in the proof of concept. The live clock is moved forward by the Analyst so genuine check-in windows can open; it creates no events. The simulation clock exists only in the demo scenario and simulates attendance and no-shows.
Check-in window
When an agent may check in for a shift: from 30 minutes before the start until 1 hour after it.
Reminder states
Created (the reminder exists) → In inbox (an in-app notification was written for an agent who has an account) → Opened (the agent opened that notification) → Checked in (the agent completed a valid check-in). Each is recorded separately; none implies another.
Notification
A message inside ShiftSense, addressed to one person, one agent, or one role within a team. It can carry a priority, a deadline and an "Open" link to the related record.
Thread / conversation
A message exchange attached to an agent, a request, an alert or a governance case. Only the members of that thread can read or post.
Card
A focused pop-up window that holds everything needed for one decision: the facts, the options, the buttons and the conversation.
Governance case
A review opened by Admin when a pattern looks unusual (for example one agent approved far more often than peers). It is a signal for review, never a finding of misconduct.
Corrective action
A formal consequence after a governance review. Admin can request it, but only Ops can authorise it.
Audit trail / hash chain
Every action writes an audit row (time, who, role, what, record, before, after, reason, outcome). Each row contains a fingerprint (SHA-256 hash) of the previous row, so changing or deleting any past row breaks the chain, and the break is reported with its row number.
Append-only
Records are only ever added. A reversal (withdraw, revoke, reject) adds a new record; it never edits the old one.
All-or-nothing change (transaction)
Every change is applied completely or not at all. If anything fails part-way, the workspace is restored exactly. A second click, or a decision on a record someone else just changed, returns a "Conflict" message instead of applying twice.
Idempotency key
A unique label per email (record + recipient). Repeating an action never sends the same email twice.
SMTP relay
The mail server that accepts outgoing email. "Sent" in ShiftSense means the relay accepted the message. It does not prove the email reached the inbox.
Verified email
An address whose owner proved it is theirs by entering a 6-digit code that was emailed to it. Codes expire after 30 minutes and are stored only as a fingerprint.
PBKDF2
A standard way to store passwords as a slow, salted fingerprint, so the password itself is never stored.
SSO (single sign-on)
Signing in with the company's own login system. Not part of the proof of concept.
Streamlit
The Python framework the app's screens are built with.
SQLite
A single-file database. ShiftSense keeps everything in data/shiftsense.db.
Seed / deterministic
The synthetic data is generated from a fixed starting number (seed 42), so every run produces exactly the same data and results.
AppTest
Streamlit's tool for running screens automatically in tests, clicking buttons and reading what appears, without a browser.
Health check / backup
Commands that check the database and configuration are sound, and create, verify or restore a copy of the database.

What every screen shares

Sidebar (left)

  • One collapse control at the top: "Collapse navigation" / "Open navigation". It works with the keyboard, keeps your page, and on phones turns the sidebar into a drawer.
  • Navigation for your role only. The Analyst and Admin have several areas (listed under their roles). Agent, Team Lead and Ops each have one screen, so the sidebar shows the screen name and an On this page list. Each item jumps to that section; the section you are reading is highlighted.
  • Workspace card: Live workspace or Demo scenario, with its clock. For the Analyst it also shows any unpublished draft.
  • Switch to demo scenario / Switch to live workspace (only when a scenario is loaded): changes what you look at. Nothing is copied or changed.
  • Account block: initials, name, role and team, then Refresh (load changes other people made) and Sign out, an Email button (your address, whether it is verified, and code confirmation), and "Updated hh:mm:ss".

Top bar

Screen title, current simulated time and scope, a status pill (for example "2 to decide"), a "Synthetic data" or "Demo scenario" tag, and the Notifications button with an unread count.

Notification centre

  • Each notification shows a priority (Critical Warning Normal Info), Action required when you must act, Overdue once its deadline passes, the sender, the time and a short text.
  • Open jumps straight to the related card (alert, request, case, conversation or check-in) and marks it read. Mark read acknowledges it without opening.
  • Notifications belong to one workspace. Demo ones show only while you view the demo and carry a "Simulated" tag. The same event never creates two notifications. When the related item is resolved, so is the notification.

Cards, history and accessibility

  • Cards: clicking a row, tile or button opens a focused pop-up with everything needed for one decision. Closing it returns you to where you were.
  • History: every role has a history view showing only what it is allowed to see. It has filters (period, type, search, order), pages of 25, a details view for any row, and a CSV export of exactly what you see (the export itself is logged).
  • Accessibility: visible keyboard focus, reduced motion when the device asks for it, status colours checked at 4.5:1 contrast or better in light and dark, a text summary and a data table for every chart, and headings and section links with accessible names. A full screen-reader test has not been done.

Job. Work their shifts and keep their own record right. Ask for time off or swaps, report when they can't come in, answer change requests from their team lead (such as covering a gap), and check in when reminded.

What the screen shows

  • Tiles: On shift now or Next shift (time, shift, queue, "report by" 15 minutes before), hours this week against the 50-hour limit, shifts this week, items to answer, open requests.
  • Buttons: Request time off, Swap a shift, Can't come in, Leave now (only while on shift), Message team lead, Type a request, Report a record problem.

  • My requests: what was asked, its status, who acts next, the decision due date and any reason.
  • My history: only the agent's own records: schedule changes, requests and decisions, reminders and check-ins, notifications and their own messages.

Decisions the agent owns

  • Accept or decline a change addressed to them.
  • Agree or refuse a colleague's swap request.
  • Withdraw their own pending request.

Changes the agent can make

  • Submit time-off and swap requests (one open request per day).
  • Report their own absence or early leave.
  • Check in for their own shift.
  • Send messages and record-problem reports to their team lead.

What the agent cannot do (refused on the server)

  • Report absence for anyone else, answer items addressed to someone else, or see other agents' requests or history.
  • Approve anything, or see team, cost or planning dashboards.
  • Check in for someone else, outside the window, or twice.

Receives

Change requests (cover, extend, swap, open shift), answers to their requests, revoked approvals, schedule updates when a new plan is published, check-in reminders (in-app, plus an email copy when email is configured and their address is verified), and messages from their team lead.

What the screen shows

  • Tiles: alerts to decide, changes awaiting agents' answers, requests waiting, items escalated to Ops, on shift now, coverage tomorrow (planned), overdue items.

The alert decision card (where most decisions happen)

  • Severity, state, date, alert id and team. Tiles for gap (people-hours), SLA exposure, starts in, fix cost and gap covered.
  • A staffing chart for the affected queue (required against scheduled) and any window still uncovered.
  • Options compared: the recommended fix against alternatives on coverage, cost, overtime and people touched, with why the others weren't chosen.
  • Steps: each concrete action. Donor queues are marked "stay at or above their own requirement (protected)".
  • Confidence and the number of rule-checked candidates. Changes and confirmations: what each agent has answered so far.
  • Why this fix · trade-offs: the engine's own figures (gap, steps, cost, overtime, anything left uncovered), shown instantly with no AI.
  • A conversation thread shared with Ops.

Deadline that watches the team lead

A Critical alert left undecided for 2 simulated hours is flagged to Ops once, as "Overdue at team lead". The 2 hours is a setting (tl_overdue_hours in config/policy.yaml).

Ops (operations head)

Screen: OperationsExample: ops, Anil Desai, all teamsPermissions: Ops decisions · approve requests · report spikes · view audit · all teams · message

Job. Run the next 72 hours across all teams. Decide what team leads could not fix, set follow-ups, answer the Analyst's risk flags, authorise or decline corrective action from governance, and watch recovery performance and cost.

What the screen shows (top to bottom)

  • Operations now (next 72 h): required and scheduled people-hours, coverage %, shortage, SLA exposure, surplus, absenteeism over 7 days, escalations, items with team leads, alerts resolved.
  • Decide first: up to three alerts, always visible, each as Issue → Impact → Recommended action → Expected result. The action and figures are the recovery engine's own (no AI). Open goes straight to that alert. Escalated items say "Ops decision needed".
  • Where to intervene first: a ranked table (severity, issue, team, owner, start, hours to start, gap, exposure, cheapest full fix in ₹) and a risk matrix plotting exposure against time to start.
  • Staffing variance by half-hour for today or tomorrow: required (forecast), scheduled, checked in (recorded), and started with no record (unknown, never counted as present or absent).
  • Follow-ups assigned (status Open, Seen or Done), Risk carried after Ops decisions, and Governance referrals when there are any.
  • Recovery performance: alerts raised, covered, closed with risk carried, median time-to-cover, accepted as recommended (and modified or rejected counts), escalations, uncovered people-hours now. Forecast accuracy shows "Unavailable" because there is no actual call data.
  • Demand vs supply and a coverage heat-map by queue and day, with a watch list of queue-days under 95% covered.
  • Cost (simulated, configured rates): planned labour, overtime, recovery spend, the next 3 days projected, and budget ("Not configured" unless set). Unresolved gaps are shown in people-hours, with no rupee value, because no penalty rate is configured.
  • Operations history across all teams (no account administration, no private messages).

Analyst

Screens: Planning · Data hub · DemoExample: analyst, Meera Iyer (or demo)Permissions: re-plan · all teams · manage data · manage demo · run clocks · report situations · flag risk to Ops

Job. Forecasting, planning, publishing and demo handling. Understand demand and staffing, compare the live roster with the manual and optimised plans, test what-ifs, move a plan from draft to published, run the demo scenario and clocks, import data, and tell Ops about material risks. The Analyst does not take Team Lead or Ops decisions.

Planning follows the workspace chosen in the sidebar (live or demo). Review & publish always concerns the live roster. A draft made from a demo scenario is created for the live roster and labelled with the scenario it came from. Days already started are frozen.

Planning · Demand & staffing

  • Pick a day and queue. Tiles: calls forecast; required, scheduled and productive people-hours (after 30% shrinkage); shortage; surplus; skilled agents (main and cover); SLA target.
  • A chart of people per half-hour: forecast band, required, scheduled. A gaps table; clicking a row opens the interval card listing who is on the queue and who is trained but off.
  • The forecast method is stated on screen: same weekday over the last 4 weeks, about an 80% band.

Planning · Roster & optimization

  • Manual baseline vs optimised plan vs live roster over the open days.
  • A hard-rule checklist from the independent rule checker, a heat-map of where the live roster is short (days × queues, in people-hours), and an agent picker that opens a card explaining each assignment.

Planning · What-if

  • Inputs: name, demand change by queue (−30% to +50%), extra absences, headcount change, maximum nights a week.
  • Run scenario re-optimises a copy; the live roster is untouched. It compares "Live plan", "keep plan" and "re-planned" on coverage, uncovered hours, exposure, overtime, cost, fairness and violations. Each finding is marked Proven or Likely.
  • Use as draft turns a result into a plan draft. The last 4 scenarios are kept on screen. Each run is written to the audit trail as a summary (full result sets are not stored).

Planning · Review & publish

  • Plans list and New draft (re-optimise open days). Lifecycle: draft → Validate → validated → Publish → published; the previous plan becomes superseded. Discard is possible before publishing.
  • A checklist and tiles comparing the draft with live (coverage, uncovered hours, exposure, overtime, labour cost, shifts changed and agents affected, violations). A change preview lists every changed shift.
  • Publishing needs the box "I reviewed the changes and affected agents" ticked (checked on the server too) and zero violations, re-checked at the moment of publishing. Publishing is refused if the live roster changed since the draft was made (stale draft).
  • Affected agents get their before → after shift; every team lead gets a summary.
  • Roll back: pick a superseded version, give a reason, Restore as draft. It then goes through validate, review and publish like any plan, keeps days already started, and is audited.
  • If the optimiser fails, the reason is given in plain words (no valid roster, out of time, internal error) with the blocking rule and safe next steps. The live roster stays as it is.
  • Also here: original plan vs live roster for past days, and the reproducible recovery benchmark (about 20 s).

Data input, output and flow

Reference data is the information ShiftSense plans from: employees, employment contracts, client service levels, company rules, leave, call volumes and special situations. Every change to it is a new version with an effective date (the day it starts to count); nothing is overwritten. The full agreement behind this is the Data Blueprint.

Base input data

The minimum data ShiftSense needs to run, shipped as seven ordinary files in basedata/small/ (default: 50 employees, 5 teams, 4 queues for 2 fictional clients) and basedata/full/ (120 employees in 8 teams, 6 queues for 3 clients), each with 3 contract types (Full-time 40h, Part-time 24h, Evening-Night 40h), 23 rules, leave, 8 weeks of half-hourly call volumes and 2 example situations. They load through the same converter as any upload. Load base data stores them; Rebuild live workspace from reference data builds a new optimised roster from them and makes it live (a verified backup is taken first, and it needs explicit confirmation). After a rebuild, every data change flows into the live roster automatically. Any record can later be replaced or removed.

How a change spreads

Submit→Impact preview→Approve (where needed)→Apply on the effective date→Re-check roster and staffing need→Alerts / re-plan recommendation / notices→Log
  • Impact preview: new, changed and removed records; alerts that today and tomorrow would get; later shifts that would be released; staffing gap before and after.
  • Today and tomorrow: shifts a change makes impossible (a removed skill, a leaver, a stricter contract, a disaster) become alerts for the team lead with a ready fix, exactly like an absence. A rise in staffing need (stricter service level, campaign, outage) where the roster is now short becomes an alert too.
  • Later days: impossible shifts are released and the Analyst gets a Re-plan recommended notice; the new draft still goes through validate, review and publish.
  • Never touched: shifts already started. A limit tightened mid-week applies from now on; nights already worked are history.
  • Future-dated changes are stored as scheduled and take effect when the live clock reaches their date.
  • Adjustable timings: how many days ahead get alerts after a data change (0–3, default 1) is a rule; each open-ended situation has its own reminder interval (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 or 12 hours); follow-ups take 2, 4, 8 or 24 hours; the team-lead deadline before Ops is told is a rule.
  • Drop folder: HR and telephony exports placed in data/inbox/<kind>/ are processed from the app with one click (applied at once), or by a night-time server run (python -m shiftsense.dropfolder run --as <user>), which stages them for the app to apply on its next refresh. Usable rows apply, held-back rows come back as a rows-to-fix file, and nothing is deleted.
  • Dates: each upload or paste says whether its dates are day first (default) or month first.
  • Approvals: contract changes from HR and rule or service-level changes from the Analyst need Ops; a team lead's proposal needs HR; governance thresholds proposed by Admin need Ops; continuity mode needs Ops. Nobody approves their own change, and a rejection needs a reason.

Contracts

Each employee links to a contract. A contract can only be stricter than the company-wide rules: fewer weekly hours or shifts, fewer nights, no nights, only some shift types, more rest. The optimiser, the manual baseline, recovery and the independent rule checker all apply each person's own contract.

Situations

Quick report: type a few sentences ("Dialler down for Claims-EN since 10:30, 40% capacity lost"); the form fills itself with what the text says and asks for the rest. Rules read it; the AI helps only if the rules recognise nothing. Active situations can be updated as they develop (end, effect, reminder interval), appear on a calendar with scheduled data changes, and show as a banner on every screen while they affect today. A temporary rule exception (for example two overtime days in a festival week) is a situation type too: Ops approves it and it lapses at its end.

Holiday, planned or unplanned outage, protest or strike, disaster, campaign, training or meeting, temporary rule exception, other. Only what is always known is mandatory (what, where, when it starts); the end may be "not known yet". Effects: volume change, queues closed, capacity lost, handle time change, people unavailable. In a disaster, affected teams are marked unavailable and never asked to cover; Ops can lower the service target for a period (continuity mode, which needs an end date). In a protest nobody is assumed absent unless named or expected absence is 100%. Active situations are listed for Ops and the Analyst and can be closed.

Workflows, step by step

A. Absence → cover

Agent: Can't come in (or no-show / leave now / sent home)→Gap and severity calculated→Fixes generated, filtered, simulated, ranked→Alert to the team lead
  1. Approve or Modify: affected agents get a Proposed change. Accept → resolved. Decline → the team lead gets the next-best fix automatically.
  2. Reject: a new fix is proposed that excludes the rejected actions.
  3. Escalate: Ops gets a Critical notice due in 2 h and decides (overtime budget, outside cover, accept risk) or returns it.
  4. If a Critical alert waits 2 simulated hours undecided, Ops is told once ("Overdue at team lead").

B. Time-off request

Agent asks→Coverage check→Covered: approved at once|Thin / Short: to team lead→Approve / Decline (reason)→Agent told (+ email copy if configured)

For a Short day the agent is first offered alternatives (another day, a swap, a different shift). Pending requests can be withdrawn. Approved ones can be revoked with a reason until the day starts. Requests can never change a day that has started.

C. Shift swap

Agent picks a trained colleague→Colleague agrees→Team lead approves→Roster updated, rules re-checked

D. Call spike

Ops reports spike→Gap calculated→Alert to the owning team lead, or "absorbed"

E. Attendance reminder and check-in

Team lead spots a shift needing attention→Send reminder (in-app; email copy if configured and verified)→Agent opens ShiftSense→Check in now→Server checks identity, own shift, window, not already recorded→Attendance recorded as "checked in" and audited→Team lead sees it
  • The window opens 30 minutes before the start and closes 1 hour after it. Too early: "not open yet". Too late: "closed … ask your team lead to confirm".
  • A reminder never records attendance; opening it records "opened" only. After "Send again", only the newest reminder per shift can be used.
  • If the agent couldn't check in but is working, the team lead uses Confirm presence (reason required, never overwrites). If the agent thinks a record is wrong, they use Report a record problem, which changes nothing until the team lead acts.
  • Reminder, opened, check-in and outcome stay separate in history.

F. Escalated gap closed with risk

Ops decides without new cover→Alert "Closed by Ops · N people-h still uncovered"→Ops "Risk carried" list and performance "closed with risk carried"

An Ops decision records what was decided but never changes the roster, so the gap is never reported as covered. The uncovered hours are also written into the alert's history.

G. Re-plan and publish

New draft or what-if (live or demo)→Validate (rule checker)→Review changes, tick confirmation→Publish (stale check, violations re-checked)→Affected agents and team leads notified

H. Roll back to an earlier version

Pick a superseded version + reason→Restore as draft→Validate→Review→Publish

I. Governance case

Admin scans → Flagged→Acknowledge → Under Review→Request explanation (24 h)→Team lead explains→Resolve / Dismiss / Close, or refer to Ops→Ops authorises, declines or resolves

J. Import

Upload → match columns → row-by-row check → preview and confirm → build roster and rule check → replace the live workspace atomically and audit. Any rejected row stops the import; if no valid roster can be built, the old workspace stays live and the reason is shown.

K. Demo lifecycle

Load demo story (sandbox)→Explore forecast→Simulate disruption (clock)→Evaluate recovery→What-if → Use as draft (live)→Validate → Review → Publish deliberately→Unload scenario

Until that final Publish, the live roster is unchanged; tests prove this.

L. Account change and email verification

Admin edits account→Validation (unique IDs and email, real agent, team)→Version check→Saved + audited→Takes effect on the person's next action
Person: sidebar → Email → Send code→6-digit code emailed (needs email configured)→Enter code → verified

Decision support and AI

Decision support is plain calculation, not AI. The recovery engine works out the options, costs and remaining gaps. The screens then restate those exact figures instantly:

  • Team Lead: "Why this fix · trade-offs" on every alert.
  • Ops: "Decide first", whose action is always the engine's own recommendation (so the two can never disagree), with Open to jump to the alert.
  • Admin: "Week in brief", built from the weekly report's figures.

The AI is used only where rules genuinely can't cope, and only after the rules have tried:

  • Reading a free-text request the rules can't classify, or can't find the day in.
  • Answering an open question that the rule answers don't cover (the "Ask" boxes).

The AI never approves, changes a roster or sends anything. A person always clicks. Without an AI key, or if the AI is slow (20-second limit, one retry), unreachable or returns something unexpected, the rule-based answer is used. Every answer says whether it came from the AI or from the screen's data.

Efficiency: structured jobs (reading a typed request, matching unknown columns, reading pasted text) use a smaller, faster model; open questions use the larger one. Every call has a 20-second limit and one retry, and an identical request is answered from memory instead of calling the model again. There is no trained machine-learning model in ShiftSense: the forecast is a transparent seasonal average and plans come from a mathematical optimiser, so every number can be traced.

Never decided by AI: forecasting, Erlang C, staffing, roster optimisation, rule checks, recovery options and ranking, costs, approvals, escalations, permissions, governance thresholds and case states, audit, attendance and delivery status.

Rules and the recovery engine

Hard rules (never broken)

  • Trained queues only
  • One shift per day
  • Rest ≥ 11 h between shifts
  • ≤ 50 paid hours a week
  • ≤ 6 shifts a week (≥ 1 weekly off)
  • Approved leave respected
  • Nights only for night-permitted agents, ≤ 4 a week
  • ≤ 1 overtime day a fortnight
  • ≤ 11 h daily span; extensions ≤ 2 h

Recovery actions

  • Cross-skill redeploy (donor queue protected)
  • Shift swap (partner trained for the same queue)
  • Open shift (first trained agent to accept)
  • Extension / overtime
  • If nothing closes the gap, the remaining risk is escalated, never silently accepted

Ranking

Extra cost + remaining service risk + blast radius. Started days are frozen; the smallest change wins.

After every plan, every recovery and every change, the independent rule checker re-verifies the roster. A change that would add a violation is rolled back at the moment it is saved. If the solver fails or runs out of time, nothing changes and the whole gap is escalated with an explanation.

Audit, history, integrity

  • Every action through the permission layer writes an audit row, linked in a SHA-256 hash chain. Ops and Admin can verify the chain and download it. Nobody can edit it.
  • Reversals add new records; nothing is overwritten. Audit, notifications, messages and cases survive a workspace reset.
  • Changes are all-or-nothing under a lock. Double clicks and stale decisions return a conflict instead of applying twice.
  • History draws only on stored records: audit rows, schedule changes, alert histories, system events, reminders and email attempts, notifications, messages the person may read, and governance case events. Scope comes from the stored account, never from what the browser asks for: asking for another agent or team is refused. Exports never contain check-in links, verification codes or full email addresses.
  • Health check (python -m shiftsense.health): database integrity, the six roles, accounts, live roster with zero violations, demo state, audit chain, optimiser, email configured or not, AI key presence, disk space. Backup (python -m shiftsense.backup create | verify | restore). An error log records denied actions without secrets.
