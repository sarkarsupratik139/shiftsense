ShiftSense HR Workflow Design

How HR information enters ShiftSense, gets checked, triggers decisions, updates records and reaches reports, across the employee lifecycle of a call-centre workforce. Written so a developer can build each workflow from it. Scope is HR only: operational planning, recovery and Ops decisions stay as they are.

Draft 1 · 19 Oct 2026 · context: 50 simulated agents, 4 queues, 5 teams, 14-day plan · companion to the Handbook and Data Blueprint

Scope and labels

Every capability below carries one of four labels. Nothing marked New or External exists today.

LabelMeaning
Exists · verifiedBuilt and covered by automated tests (470 on SQLite; the suite also runs on PostgreSQL).
Exists · partialBuilt, but not to the standard this design asks for. The gap is stated.
NewProposed here, to be built.
ExternalNeeds a system ShiftSense does not have: HRIS, payroll, identity provider, badge system, document store.
ShiftSense holds no leave balances, salaries, payroll, recruitment, performance reviews or disciplinary records, and this design does not invent them. Where a workflow needs one of these, it either reads it from an external system or reports it as "Not tracked".

HR and Team Lead are separate roles

HR and Team Lead have separate role identifiers, screens, data queries and server-side permissions. Neither inherits the other's rights, and neither inherits Ops' or the Analyst's.

Proof today. The permission test calls every protected server action directly, as each of the six roles: 184 combinations, all passing. That includes HR being refused all Team Lead actions (report absence, decide alerts and requests, send reminders, confirm attendance, post on an agent's private thread), and Team Leads being refused all HR actions (change another team's employee, change contracts, record HR leave, decide proposals). Forged sessions (HR claiming Team Lead, Team Lead claiming HR) are refused because identity is re-read from the stored account. Each role is also refused the other's screens.

HR flow. Employee information or formal HR request → HR validation → policy and eligibility checks → authorised HR decision → HR record update → notification → audit and HR reporting.
Team Lead flow. Team attendance event or request → Team Lead review → team-level checks → operational decision or escalation → roster or attendance update where authorised → employee notified → operational history.
Shared flow (absence). Team Lead records the absence → staffing gap evaluated → existing recovery covers it → the absence record is visible to HR → HR does any employee follow-up. Information is shared; permissions are not.

A · Employee lifecycle and master data

B · Attendance and absenteeism

Four facts are always kept apart, and none is inferred from another: scheduled (the roster says the person should work), recorded (check-in, team-lead confirmation or feed login), reported absence (someone said they are not coming) and confirmed no-show (the team lead confirms the person did not come and did not report). A shift that started with no record is "no attendance record": unknown, never absent.

  • Late arrival: minutes after start from check-in or feed (check-in is known to the half-hour, the feed to the minute). Lateness of 1 minute or more is recorded; a threshold for "counts as late" is an HR policy value (default 5 min).
  • Early departure: the team lead's "send someone home" or the agent's "leave now" (existing), or a feed logout earlier than the shift end minus the tolerance (new, recorded only; no automatic alert).
  • Absence category: reported reasons map to HR categories: Unwell → Sickness, Family emergency → Emergency, Other → Unspecified; approved leave keeps its leave type. HR may re-categorise with a reason (new).
  • Finalisation (new): 48 hours after the shift end the day's attendance is finalised. Before that, the team lead can still confirm. After that, only an HR-approved correction changes it.
  • Pattern review (new): a rule (default: 3 or more unplanned absences or no-shows in 30 days, or 2 Monday/Friday absences in 4 weeks) creates an HR review item. It is a signal for a conversation, never a finding or a sanction, and it is visible only to HR.
  • Synchronisation: an absence creates the operational alert (existing). The alert closing never changes the attendance fact, and the attendance fact never closes the alert.

D · Shift changes and overtime

Two records are kept apart. The operational decision (how a gap is covered) belongs to the Team Lead and Ops and lives in alerts and the roster. The HR record (who worked how many hours, overtime, compliance) is derived from the confirmed roster and kept in an overtime ledger.

  • The overtime ledger entry is written in the same transaction as the agent's confirmation. A declined offer writes nothing.
  • Compliance (rest at least 11 hours, weekly hours, shifts, nights, overtime days, per contract) is enforced before the change (existing). The HR report shows actual values against limits, plus any approved temporary rule exceptions.
  • Policy option (new, off by default): overtime above N hours a month for one person needs HR sign-off after the operational approval. This is the only case where HR approves overtime.
  • Notifications: the agent gets before and after (existing); the team lead gets confirmation; HR gets a monthly overtime summary, not each event.

E · Employee concerns and HR cases

ConfidentialityWho sees detailsWhat a Team Lead or Ops may see
Standard (policy query, general concern)HR, the employeeNothing, unless HR shares an action they must take
Restricted (grievance about a person, health)The assigned caseworker and senior HR, the employeeOnly an action, without the reason: "Schedule A12 on Morning shifts only until 30 Nov". The referring team lead sees "received" and "closed".

Why: managers need to act on outcomes (a schedule restriction), not to know reasons. Ops needs to know only when an outcome affects coverage, and then only the effect. Every view of a restricted case is itself logged.

Reused as they are: EMPLOYEE_VERSION (ref_records), ACCOUNT, CONTRACT, ROSTER_SHIFT (workspace plus live_roster), ALERT, NOTICE, AUDIT_LOG, change sets. Reused with additions: LEAVE_REQUEST (today the workspace's request list; becomes a table with type and HR stage). New: ATTENDANCE_EVENT, ATTENDANCE_CORRECTION, LEAVE_TYPE, LEAVE_BALANCE, HR_APPROVAL, OVERTIME_RECORD, HR_CASE, HR_CASE_EVENT, HR_CASE_EVIDENCE, HR_POLICY.

Record kinds. A current-state record is the latest version (employee, balance). A historical event is append-only and never edited (attendance event, overtime record, case event, audit row). A pending request is a record whose status can still move (leave request, correction). An approved decision is an HR_APPROVAL row plus the events it caused; it is reversed only by a new reversing event.

Existing fields (id, agent_id, day or range, coverage status, state, alternative, note, reason) plus: leave_type · date_from · date_to · hr_review_required (bool) · hr_review_reason · clarification_question · version (for conflict checks).

HR_APPROVAL (new)

id · subject_type (leave_request, correction, overtime) · subject_id · stage (operational, policy) · decided_by · role · decision (approve, reject, clarify) · reason · decided_at. Unique (subject_type, subject_id, stage, decision_round), which prevents repeated approvals.

OVERTIME_RECORD (new)

id · employee_id · shift_date · kind (extension, extra shift) · hours · rate · multiplier · approved_by (TL) · confirmed_at (agent) · alert_id · reversal_of. Written in the same transaction as the agent's confirmation.

HR_CASE, HR_CASE_EVENT, HR_CASE_EVIDENCE (new)

HR_CASE_EVIDENCE: id · case_id · filename · content_hash · stored_ref (document store) · uploaded_by · created. Files live outside the database; only references and hashes are stored.

  • All checks run in services on every call, with identity re-read from the stored account. Hiding a button is never the protection.
  • Every consequential change writes an audit row (actor, role, time, action, record, before, after, reason) to the hash-chained, database-protected audit trail. Views of restricted cases are logged too.

11 · Errors, rollback, reconciliation, recovery

  • All or nothing. Multi-record changes run in one transaction. For example, approving leave writes the approval, leave days, roster release, attendance expectation, balance and notices together, or nothing. This uses the existing transaction pattern (workspace snapshot plus a database transaction).
  • Duplicates. Unique keys stop repeats: one presence event per shift, one approval per stage and round, one open request per day, an idempotency key per email, and dedupe keys on notices. A double click returns "already done" instead of applying twice (existing pattern).
  • Conflicting updates. Version checks: an employee save from a stale screen is refused with "changed by X at Y, reload" (existing). Two servers can't overwrite each other's workspace (existing).
  • Corrections without loss. Nothing is edited in place. Corrections and reversals are new events pointing at what they correct (supersedes_id, reversal_of).
  • Failure part-way. If a downstream step fails (for example the roster check), the whole approval rolls back and the approver sees why. The request stays Under review.
  • Reconciliation (nightly).
    • Approved leave days match roster gaps.
    • Exited employees have no future shifts and no active account.
    • Overtime ledger totals match roster extensions.
    • HRIS balances match ShiftSense pending and taken days.
    • Any mismatch becomes an HR data-quality item; nothing is fixed silently.
  • Recovery. Verified backups with restore drill (existing). A failed integration file is moved to "failed" with a rows-to-fix file (existing drop folder).
